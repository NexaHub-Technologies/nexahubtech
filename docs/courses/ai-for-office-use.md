# AI for Office Use

The practical introduction to artificial intelligence for administrative, clerical and
office workers. Prompt engineering from scratch, and how to apply it across Microsoft
Word, Excel and PowerPoint to get real work done in less time.

> **The mental model — AI is a fast collaborator that needs clear context and direction**
>
> Artificial intelligence does not guess your mind; it predicts the most useful
> continuation based on the context and instructions you provide. Treat an AI assistant
> like an eager junior colleague: explain the goal, define the constraints, show an
> example, and verify the output. Once you master structured prompting and apply it
> directly within Word, Excel and PowerPoint, tasks that used to consume hours turn into
> minutes of thoughtful editing.

## Before you start

**Know:** basic computer literacy — opening files, browsing the web, and everyday use
of Microsoft Office (Word, Excel, email). No programming, mathematical training, or
prior experience with AI models is required.

**Have:** a laptop or desktop computer with internet access and Microsoft Office
installed (or access to Office on the web). 3–5 hours a week outside sessions to
practise prompt engineering and exercises.

> **On hallucinations.** An AI will confidently invent a total or a reference if you
> do not tell it how to behave. This course teaches how to constrain output, verify
> results against source data, and use AI to speed up thinking rather than abdicate
> responsibility.

## Module 01 — AI fundamentals and prompt engineering
*Week 1*

How generative AI works, system prompts, role prompting, context windows, few-shot
examples, and avoiding hallucinations.

**By the end you can**
- Structure prompts using role, context, task, constraints and output format.
- Use few-shot prompting to force exact styles and data structures.
- Spot and stop hallucinations before unverified text reaches a client or manager.
- Choose between web AI assistants and integrated tools for a given office job.

**The misconception this clears.** That prompt engineering is asking conversational
questions and hoping for the best. Good prompting is specification: giving the model
clear boundaries, explicit rules, and sample input/output pairs.

**Exercise brief.** You are given five vague, messy prompts typical of beginners.
Rewrite all five into structured professional prompts that produce reliable,
reproducible corporate answers. Test both versions side by side and document the
differences in output quality.

**Model answer demonstrates.** Prompts structured with explicit role definitions,
source constraints, negative constraints (what *not* to say), and structured output
formats like tables and bulleted executive summaries.

## Module 02 — AI for Microsoft Word and corporate documents
*Week 2*

Drafting formal letters, reports, executive summaries, tone shifting, grammar
refinement, and summarizing long PDFs.

**By the end you can**
- Draft initial reports, formal memos, and customer letters from brief notes in minutes.
- Shift document tone between formal administrative, executive brief, and conversational.
- Extract key actions and decisions from a twenty-page meeting transcript or PDF.
- Edit AI-generated drafts into accurate human-authored final copy.

**The misconception this clears.** That AI text should be copied and pasted directly
into the company letterhead. AI produces a fast first draft; the professional skill is
steering the draft, validating facts, and applying company style.

**Exercise brief.** Take a messy five-page meeting transcript or set of handwritten
rough notes. Using AI, generate three distinct deliverables: a one-page executive memo
for management, an itemized action-point checklist for staff, and a formal client update
letter.

**Model answer demonstrates.** Clear tone differentiation across the three documents,
complete retention of numerical and factual details, and elimination of generic AI
filler phrases.

## Module 03 — AI for Microsoft Excel and data analysis
*Week 3*

Prompting for formulas (XLOOKUP, nested IFs), debugging spreadsheet errors, extracting
data from messy text, and trend summaries.

**By the end you can**
- Describe a spreadsheet logic problem in plain English and receive the exact working
  Excel formula.
- Diagnose and fix `#N/A`, `#VALUE!`, and circular reference errors by pasting formulas
  into an AI helper.
- Clean and normalize messy pasted text into tabular CSV or table rows using AI.
- Ask natural language analytical questions about a sales or inventory table.

**The misconception this clears.** That you must memorize complex formula syntax to
solve advanced spreadsheet problems. The skill is accurately describing your table
structure, columns, and logic condition to the AI.

**Exercise brief.** You are given a spreadsheet with three broken formulas and an
unstructured text block of customer addresses and phone numbers. Use structured AI
prompting to debug all three formulas, explain why each failed, and extract the text
block into clean, column-ready Excel data.

**Model answer demonstrates.** Fixed formulas using robust functions (`XLOOKUP`, `INDEX/MATCH`,
`IFERROR`), with clear explanations of root causes and perfectly formatted tabular
data ready for Excel pasting.

## Module 04 — AI for presentations, productivity and workplace ethics
*Week 4*

Outlining PowerPoint presentations, drafting email responses, meeting minute synthesis,
data privacy, and workplace verification.

**By the end you can**
- Generate complete multi-slide deck outlines with slide titles, bullet points, and
  speaker notes from a project brief.
- Draft polite, professional email replies to difficult or complex inquiries.
- Understand corporate data privacy: what sensitive data must never be pasted into public
  AI models.
- Apply a verification checklist before any AI-assisted work is signed off.

**The misconception this clears.** That using AI in the office is cheating or unsafe.
When used transparently, with data privacy respected and outputs verified, AI is a standard
productivity tool like a calculator or a spellchecker.

**Exercise brief.** Given a brief for a new company initiative, generate a 6-slide
presentation outline with speaker notes. Then draft two email communications announcing
it to different audiences. Finally, complete a risk assessment identifying any sensitive
data points that required anonymization.

**Model answer demonstrates.** High-impact presentation narrative structure, clear
audience-specific email tone, and an explicit privacy checklist demonstrating safe
corporate tool usage.

## Assessment across the course

No grades, and no course project. Assessment is continuous through the weekly exercise
briefs, focusing on whether students produce accurate, verified, and professional
workplace artefacts with measurable time savings.
