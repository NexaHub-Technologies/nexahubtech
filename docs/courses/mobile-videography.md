# Mobile Videography

Shoot, light, record and edit video worth watching — on the phone you already own.

> **The mental model — The edit starts when you press record**
>
> On a phone, the camera is never the limit — the decisions are. Where you stand, what
> is in the frame, where the light comes from and how close the microphone is are all
> settled before you press record, and no editing app can fix them afterwards. Shoot as
> if you are already in the edit: hold each shot long enough to cut, get the sound
> clean, and film the moment before and after. Then editing stops being rescue work and
> becomes what it should be — choosing.

## Before you start

**Know:** no filming or editing experience. How to install an app and move files on your
phone.

**Have:** a phone that records at 1080p or better, with 10GB free. A free phone editing
app — we say which in the first session. For online sessions, a stable internet
connection. 3–4 hours a week for shooting and editing assignments.

## Module 01 — The phone as a camera
*Week 1*

Framing, focus and exposure lock, holding it steady, and shooting so the clips will cut.

**By the end you can**
- Lock focus and exposure so the phone stops hunting mid-shot.
- Hold the phone steady without expensive stabilisers.
- Film five continuous seconds of usable footage before and after any action.

**Exercise brief.** Pick an ordinary subject and shoot five separate ten-second clips
demonstrating locked exposure and intentional framing. Record in daylight with zero camera
shake.

**Model answer demonstrates.** Smooth framing, stable horizon line, and stable exposure
that remains constant when the subject moves.

---

## Module 02 — Light and sound you already have
*Week 2*

Windows, shade and a quiet room: clean light and clean audio without buying kit.

**By the end you can**
- Position a subject relative to window light to avoid harsh shadows and glare.
- Position the phone's microphone close enough to record intelligible speech.
- Eliminate ambient room echo using common household materials.

**Exercise brief.** Record a two-minute spoken explanation in a room with background noise,
first untreated, then using directional placement and acoustic damping. Compare the two
recordings.

**Model answer demonstrates.** Clear vocal separation, balanced lighting across the speaker's
face, and no wind or fan distortion.

---

## Module 03 — Editing on the phone
*Week 3*

Cutting, pacing, captions and music in a free editing app — and the decisions behind them.

**By the end you can**
- Assemble a rough cut on mobile timeline software.
- Trim head and tail footage to keep pacing brisk.
- Add readable on-screen subtitles and adjust background music levels.

**Exercise brief.** Take the clips filmed in modules 01 and 02 and assemble a 45-second
reel with transitions, clean cuts on action, and synced captions.

**Model answer demonstrates.** Natural cutting rhythm, audio ducking under speech, and
high-resolution export settings.

---

## Module 04 — A short for a real purpose
*Week 4*

Plan, shoot, edit and export a finished short for a business, a church or an event.

**By the end you can**
- Storyboard and film a complete promotional or narrative short on mobile.
- Colour-correct and grade footage directly in mobile editing software.
- Export optimal aspect ratios and bitrates for Instagram, TikTok and YouTube.

**Exercise brief.** Plan, record, and finish a 60-second video for a local business,
event or product. Deliver the final export ready for social publication.

**Model answer demonstrates.** Complete narrative arc, balanced audio mix, accurate color,
and proper vertical or widescreen export formatting.

## Assessment across the course

No grades, and no course project. Assessment is based on the quality, framing and
storytelling demonstrated across the weekly exercises.
