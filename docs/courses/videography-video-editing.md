# Videography & Video Editing

Capture and craft video content that communicates — from shooting to final cut.

> **The mental model — You are directing attention, not recording events**
>
> A camera doesn't capture a scene — it excludes everything outside the frame. A
> light doesn't only brighten; it tells the eye where to go. A cut moves attention
> from one thing to the next. Once framing, lighting and editing read as three
> ways of answering the same question — what should the viewer be looking at, and
> when — technique stops being a list of settings and becomes a set of decisions.

## Before you start

**Know:** no filming, lighting or editing experience; basic computer skills —
files, folders, moving large files around.

**Have:** a camera, or a phone that records at 1080p or better; a laptop that can
edit video — 8GB RAM and 100GB free; 6–8 hours a week.

> **A phone is genuinely fine.** Every assignment here can be completed on a
> modern phone. The course is about decisions, and better equipment does not make
> better decisions.

## Projects

3 projects across the course, each built once the modules behind it are done.
They are independent pieces, not stages of one thing — project 2 starts from
nothing rather than extending project 1 — so you finish with 3 separate pieces
of work you can show somebody, each demonstrating something different.

The per-module exercise briefs below are practice. These are the integration.

| # | Modules | Weeks | The project |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 01–02 | 1–4 | A piece made in-camera, lit and recorded properly |
| 2 | 03 | 5–6 | Two cuts from one set of rushes |
| 3 | 04–05 | 7–8 | **Course capstone.** Shot, cut, graded, delivered |

### Project 1 — A piece made in-camera
*Modules 01–02 · Weeks 1–4*

One ordinary subject shot five ways, each framing telling a different story about
it, with a line per shot on what you excluded. Then a two-minute interview
recorded in a deliberately difficult room, delivered as recorded and as treated,
relit so the subject separates from the background.

**What is assessed**
- Five genuinely different readings of one subject, and the written reasons.
- An audible improvement between the two audio versions.
- Light used to separate, not merely to brighten.
- Exposure decisions you can explain as trades, not settings you found.

### Project 2 — Two cuts from one set of rushes
*Module 03 · Weeks 5–6*

A 90-second cut from supplied rushes with no music at all. Then a second cut from
the same material making the opposite point, with a paragraph on what changed.

**What is assessed**
- Pacing that holds without music doing the work.
- A genuine second reading, not a reshuffle.
- Cuts land where attention is ready to move, and you can say why.

### Project 3 — The capstone
*Modules 04–05 · Weeks 7–8*

**Course capstone.** Concept, shoot and deliver a finished piece of two to four
minutes for a real brief — a business, an event, a person. It must have a point,
be watchable on a phone with the sound off for the first ten seconds, and be
exported correctly for its platform.

The brief is issued at the end of week 6 so the concept is settled before the
shooting week opens. Colour and finishing are taught across these two weeks
against your own footage rather than supplied clips — your material is the
material that has to cut together.

**What is assessed**
- Audio is clean throughout.
- Shots are framed on purpose; you can justify each one.
- The cut holds attention without leaning on music.
- Colour is consistent across the piece.
- Exported correctly for where it will actually be watched.
- You can state the point of the piece in one sentence.

## Module 01 — Camera fundamentals
*Weeks 1–2*

Exposure, framing, and movement. How to use a camera with intention, not
guesswork.

**By the end you can**
- Control exposure deliberately — and explain the trade you made to get it.
- Frame a shot by deciding what to exclude.
- Move the camera for a reason you can state.

**The misconception this clears.** That the camera records what you see. It
records a rectangle you chose, at a brightness you chose — every frame is an
argument about what matters.

**Exercise brief.** Shoot the same ordinary subject five times, each framing
telling a different story about it. No dialogue, no captions. Then write one line
per shot: what you excluded, and why.

**Model answer demonstrates.** Five genuinely different readings of one subject —
and the written reasons, which are the point.

---

## Module 02 — Lighting & audio
*Weeks 3–4*

The invisible elements that make or break a video. Sound and light, controlled.

**By the end you can**
- Shape available light to direct the eye, with reflectors or none at all.
- Record clean audio in an imperfect room.
- Diagnose why footage looks amateur when nothing is obviously wrong.

**The misconception this clears.** That viewers forgive bad audio more than bad
picture. The opposite is true — an audience will watch a soft image and will
switch off unclear sound within seconds.

**Exercise brief.** Record a two-minute interview in a difficult room. Deliver
two versions: one with the audio as recorded, one with your best treatment.
Then relight the same setup so the subject separates from the background, using
only what you already have.

**Model answer demonstrates.** An audible improvement, and light used to
separate rather than merely to brighten.

---

## Module 03 — Editing with purpose
*Weeks 5–6*

Cuts, pacing, and flow. Software skills plus the editorial thinking behind them.

**By the end you can**
- Cut on the moment attention is ready to move.
- Build a sequence that holds without relying on music.
- Cut the same footage two ways and say what each version argues.

**The misconception this clears.** That editing is removing bad takes. It is
deciding where the viewer looks next — the cut is the most powerful tool in the
course.

**Exercise brief.** From a supplied set of rushes, cut a 90-second piece with no
music at all. Then cut a second version making the opposite point from the same
material. Write one paragraph on what changed.

**Model answer demonstrates.** Pacing that holds without music, and a genuine
second reading rather than a reshuffle.

---

## Module 04 — Color & finishing
*Week 7*

Color grading, titles, and export. Polishing your work to a professional
standard.

**By the end you can**
- Correct footage to a neutral base, then grade for intent.
- Add titles that are readable on a phone.
- Export correctly for the platform it will be watched on.

**The misconception this clears.** That grading is applying a look. Correction
comes first — a LUT on inconsistent footage makes it consistently wrong.

**Exercise brief.** Match three clips shot under different lighting so they cut
together invisibly. Then grade the sequence for a stated mood, and export for
both Instagram and YouTube with the right settings for each.

**Model answer demonstrates.** Invisible matching first — the grade is only
judged once the correction holds.

## Module 05 — A complete project
*Weeks 7–8*

Shoot, edit, and deliver a finished piece from concept to export.

> **The brief is issued at the end of week 6**, so the concept is settled and

**The project.** This module's work is Project 3, the course capstone —
specified in full in the Projects section above.

## Assessment across the course

Every module asks for a written line or paragraph alongside the footage. The
footage shows what you did; the writing shows whether you meant it.
